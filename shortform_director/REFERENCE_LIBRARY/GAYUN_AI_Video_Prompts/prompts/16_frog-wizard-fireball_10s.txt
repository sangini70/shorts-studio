Create a 10-second narrative pixel-art animated short.

CORE STYLE:
Authentic hand-crafted 16-bit pixel art animation.

Large clearly visible square pixels.
Low-resolution retro game aesthetic.
Limited color palette.
Hard pixel edges.
No anti-aliasing.
No smooth vector edges.
No realistic rendering.

Dark burgundy-black shadows,
multiple saturated greens for the frog,
bright yellow, orange and amber fire,
deep blue and cyan water.

Every frame must look like an actual sprite-based
1990s console game animation.

Characters remain consistently pixelated during movement.

NO typography.
NO subtitles.
NO UI.
NO health bars.
NO written text.

────────────────────────────
0:00–0:02.5 — THE SPELL

WIDE SHOT.

Nighttime pixel-art forest.

A tiny green frog wizard stands alone
on a dark tree stump.

Small glowing fireflies move through the background.

The frog wears a tiny dark-purple wizard hat.

He dramatically raises both hands.

Camera CUTS closer.

MEDIUM SHOT.

The frog closes his eyes and concentrates.

FROG, confidently:

"Okay... tiny fireball."

Cute but slightly raspy male cartoon voice.

A tiny orange spark appears between his hands.

PING.

The spark grows.

The frog opens one eye.

He smiles.

FROG:

"Yes!"

Retro chiptune music begins.

────────────────────────────
0:02.5–0:04.5 — TOO MUCH

CLOSE-UP.

The tiny fireball suddenly expands.

WHOOM!

It becomes a gigantic pixelated flame.

CUT.

The frog is now completely surrounded by fire.

Exactly like a dramatic retro-game sprite:
green frog in foreground,
huge yellow-orange pixel flames behind him.

His eyes become enormous.

One beat of complete silence.

FROG:

"...oh."

Suddenly his wizard hat catches fire.

FROG:

"OH! OH! OH!"

He frantically waves both arms.

Rapid sprite animation.

The fire flickers wildly.

8-bit alarm-like percussion enters the soundtrack.

────────────────────────────
0:04.5–0:06.5 — PANIC

SIDE-SCROLLING SHOT.

The burning frog runs across the forest.

His tiny legs move absurdly fast.

A trail of pixel flames follows him.

FROG:

"WATER! WATER!"

Camera tracks sideways like a classic platform game.

He jumps over a rock.

Nearly hits a tree.

Changes direction.

Runs again.

Fast comedic chiptune percussion.

Tiny retro footsteps:

TIK-TIK-TIK-TIK-TIK.

Fire:

FWOOSH—FWOOSH.

Suddenly he sees a small pond.

His eyes widen.

────────────────────────────
0:06.5–0:08 — JUMP

FRONTAL CLOSE-UP.

The frog runs directly toward camera.

FROG:

"COMING THROUGH!"

SMASH CUT:

WIDE SHOT OF POND.

The burning frog flies through the air
from left to right.

Freeze his silhouette for approximately 0.15 seconds
against the night sky.

Then—

SPLASH!

He disappears into the pixelated blue water.

Huge geometric pixel splash.

Music abruptly stops.

────────────────────────────
0:08–0:10 — PUNCHLINE

Everything becomes quiet.

Static wide shot of the pond.

Small pixel water ripples.

Crickets.

Nothing happens for approximately one second.

Then the frog's two eyes slowly rise above the water.

His entire body is underwater.

He looks exhausted.

FROG, quietly:

"...nailed it."

PING.

A tiny orange flame suddenly appears
on top of his head.

Even though he is underwater.

His eyes slowly look upward toward it.

One tiny:

"pop."

CUT TO BLACK.

PIXEL ART DIRECTION

Authentic 16-bit sprite animation.

Use deliberately limited animation frames.

Fire animation:
3–5 repeating hand-drawn pixel flame states.

Running animation:
classic 4–6 frame sprite cycle.

Water splash:
large blocky clusters of cyan and dark-blue pixels.

Facial expressions must remain extremely simple:
large white pixel eyes,
black pupils,
simple mouth shapes.

Emotions should come from
eye position,
mouth shape,
body pose,
and timing.

Do NOT introduce detailed facial anatomy.

────────────────────────────
CAMERA LANGUAGE

Despite the retro pixel-art aesthetic,
use cinematic storytelling through cuts:

wide establishing shot,
medium shot,
close-up,
side-scrolling tracking shot,
frontal shot,
wide action shot,
final static comedy shot.

Use HARD CUTS.

Occasional fast digital punch-in.

No smooth cinematic camera floating.

Camera movement should feel like
a retro game suddenly behaving like a cartoon.

────────────────────────────
AUDIO

Original playful 16-bit chiptune soundtrack.

Square-wave melody.
Simple synthesized bass.
Retro drum-machine percussion.

During panic:
music becomes faster and more chaotic.

When the frog hits the water:
music stops immediately.

Use classic game-inspired sound design:

magic spark — high-pitched PING
fire appearing — pixelated WHOOM
running — rapid tiny footsteps
jump — short ascending synth tone
water — exaggerated 8-bit SPLASH
final flame — tiny POP

Dialogue remains clear above music.

FROG VOICE:
small expressive male cartoon voice,
slightly raspy,
confident at first,
frantic in the middle,
completely exhausted at the end.

Exact dialogue:

"Okay... tiny fireball."

"Yes!"

"...oh."

"OH! OH! OH!"

"WATER! WATER!"

"COMING THROUGH!"

"...nailed it."

────────────────────────────
CRITICAL CONSISTENCY

The frog must remain the SAME character in every shot.

Same green body.
Same large white eyes.
Same dark-purple wizard hat.
Same proportions.
Same pixel resolution.
Same sprite-design language.

Do not suddenly increase visual detail during close-ups.

A close-up must simply reveal
LARGER PIXELS,
not higher-resolution artwork.

This is extremely important.

────────────────────────────
STRICTLY AVOID

3D,
photorealism,
smooth vector animation,
anti-aliased edges,
high-resolution illustration,
anime,
realistic frog anatomy,
realistic fire,
realistic water simulation,
motion blur,
depth of field,
cinematic lens effects,
text,
captions,
logos,
game interface,
health bars,
character redesign between shots.
