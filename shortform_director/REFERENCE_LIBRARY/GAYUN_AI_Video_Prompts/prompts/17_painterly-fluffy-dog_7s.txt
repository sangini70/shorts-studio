Create a 7-second whimsical hand-painted animated short.

REFERENCE STYLE:
Preserve the exact visual spirit of the reference image.

A tiny fluffy white dog painted with loose, visible oil-paint brushstrokes.
Its body is almost an abstract soft white oval made from thick layered paint.
Very minimal facial features:
tiny dark eyes,
small black nose,
tiny mouth.

The fur is represented by expressive dry-brush strokes rather than individual realistic hairs.

Background:
muted mint-green painted sky,
soft yellow-green grass,
visible woven canvas texture beneath the paint.

Every single frame must look like an oil painting on textured canvas.

IMPORTANT:
This is NOT a realistic dog video with an oil-paint filter.
The dog itself must be CONSTRUCTED FROM PAINT STROKES.

The brushstrokes should subtly animate and reshape as the dog moves.

No photorealism.
No 3D.
No clean digital illustration.
No sharp vector edges.

PAINTING & ANIMATION LANGUAGE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

CRITICAL:

Maintain visible physical canvas texture
throughout the ENTIRE video.

Use:
thick oil paint,
dry-brush marks,
soft scraped pigment,
visible bristle marks,
slightly uneven painted edges,
subtle variations in paint opacity.

The animation should feel as though
an oil painter is repainting the canvas frame by frame.

Do not create smooth digital deformation.

Instead,
motion should be expressed through CHANGING BRUSHSTROKES.

When the wind moves the fur,
individual painted strokes extend toward the right.

When the dog rolls,
the entire painted silhouette rotates and reforms
through loose expressive brush marks.

Grass should also be individual loose green strokes
that bend naturally with the wind.

Keep the composition extremely simple.

Large areas of empty mint-green canvas
are desirable.

CAMERA & EDITING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Use several clear cinematic shots despite the painterly style:

medium opening shot,
facial close-up,
wide wind shot,
side-tracking rolling shot,
dog-and-flower two-shot,
final wide composition.

Use simple HARD CUTS.

No flashy transitions.
No zoom effects.
No camera shake.

The storytelling comes from character movement
and composition.
